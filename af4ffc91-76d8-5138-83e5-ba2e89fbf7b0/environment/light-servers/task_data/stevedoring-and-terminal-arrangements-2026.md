# Stevedoring and terminal arrangements under the 2026 charters (claims desk reference)

All four 2026 charters are on terms where cargo is put on board / taken off by Charterers' arrangement:

1. Stevedores are appointed and paid by Merrow or by its shippers/receivers (clause 22 of each charter). A gang that is not
   supplied, arrives late or is short is a matter on Charterers' side of the contract.
2. Shore installations — conveyors, shiploaders, grabs, hoppers, stockpiles and the supply of cargo to the berth — are the
   terminal's, provided at Charterers' arrangement. A breakdown or a cargo shortfall at the terminal is on Charterers' side.
3. None of the four charters contains a breakdown, strike or 'cargo not ready' exception. Such stoppages are not among the
   charter exceptions and laytime continues to run through them.
4. Ship's gear: all four Q3 vessels on the ore and coal contracts are gearless; the Kestrel and Holmfirth vessels used shore
   cranes and spouts only. No ship's-gear breakdowns are recorded in Q3.
5. Costs of shore stoppages (terminal standby, extra shifts) are recovered from shippers/receivers under the sale contracts
   by the commercial team; they never appear on the laytime statement.

Terminal incident reports go to operations (Sefa Ndlovu), not to the claims desk.

Per contract:
- Aldwick COA (coal, Baltic): shippers' terminals at Ventspils, Gdansk and Riga load by conveyor and travelling
  shiploader; the stevedoring is part of the terminal service.
- Holmfirth grain programme: silo terminals at Rouen, Constanta and Ghent; cargo arrives by truck and rail and is held
  in the terminal's stockpile/silo before loading; spouts are terminal equipment.
- Kestrel fertiliser discharges: receivers' stevedores from the port labour pool; mobile harbour cranes and hoppers are
  the terminal's; night gangs are ordered by the agents on receivers' behalf.
- Tarnby ore programme: LKAB / Malmhamnen ore terminals with fixed shiploaders; ore is conveyed from the stockyard.
