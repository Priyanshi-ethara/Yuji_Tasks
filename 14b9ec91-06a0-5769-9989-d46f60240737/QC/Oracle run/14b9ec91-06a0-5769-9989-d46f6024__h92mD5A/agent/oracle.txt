In force from 1 April 2026. Applies to the 132 kV circuits on the Next-Day Ratings register. Issue 2 (seasonal ratings) is withdrawn.\n1 Purpose\n1.1 Each day the rating desk sets the continuous day-ahead rating of every circuit on the register for the next rating day, from the weather forecast for that day, the conductor, and any series element in the circuit.\n2 Sources of record\n2.1 Circuit data (conductor code, governing met stations): the Next-Day Ratings register. Earlier register exports are snapshots and are not used.\n2.2 Weather: the MetDesk station forecast service. MetDesk maintains the station forecasts live and reissues a station's forecast when the forecast for that station changes (model runs, observations, amendments). The service is the source of record; a pull, file copy, draft or handover note made from it is not.\n2.3 Series elements: ServiceNow change records. A change in state 3 (Closed) is in service. State -2 (Scheduled), -1 (Implement) and 4 (Canceled) changes do not add an element. A Closed change that removes an element takes it out of the circuit.\n2.4 Loads: the latest day-ahead load forecast issue for the rating day on the monitoring event stream (Datadog events). A later issue supersedes an earlier one.\n2.5 Bands and ratings: NGS-RT-04 Table 2 (bands) and Tables 3a-3d (ratings by conductor) on the Ratings wiki.\n3 Rating day\n3.1 The rating day runs 00:00 to 24:00 UTC. The forecast steps used are those stamped within the rating day (00:00 to 21:00 for 3-hourly steps). Steps stamped on the day before or the day after are not used.\n4 Weather inputs\n4.1 Ambient: the highest forecast air temperature of any rating-day step at the station.\n4.2 Wind: the lowest forecast wind speed of any rating-day step at the station.\n4.3 Where the register lists more than one station for a circuit, the ambient input is the highest of the stations' ambient inputs and the wind input is the lowest of the stations' wind inputs, each taken on its own (they need not come from the same station).\n4.4 Inputs are banded in the units Table 2 states. The desk records each station's inputs in the units the forecast service delivers and in the Table 2 units.\n5 Bands\n5.1 Ambient band T1-T6 and wind band W1-W4 per Table 2, lower limit inclusive, upper limit exclusive. Labels are written exactly as Table 2 gives them.\n6 Line rating\n6.1 The overhead-line rating is read from the Table 3 page of the conductor the register gives for the circuit, at the circuit's ambient band (row) and wind band (column). Conductor codes are matched exactly.\n7 Series elements\n7.1 Where a series element is in service in the circuit, the circuit rating is the lower of the overhead-line rating and the element's continuous rating, and the limiting element is the one with the lower rating: 'Overhead line' or 'Cable section'. Without a series element the limiting element is 'Overhead line'.\n8 Constraint\n8.1 A circuit is constrained for the rating day when its forecast peak load (clause 2.4) is 90 % or more of its issued rating.\n9 Issue\n9.1 The desk writes each circuit's ambient band, wind band, rating and limiting element on the register, then posts the day-ahead notice in #grid-control and mails it to the System Operator on duty (the SO desk on-call). The notice format is on the Control Room Operations wiki.\n9.2 The issued ratings are those on the register when the notice is posted.\n10 Out of scope for the rating desk\n10.1 Protection settings (relay pickups, setting groups) - Protection Engineering under P-11.\n10.2 Public and customer outage notices - Customer Communications.\n10.3 Rolling a previous day's ratings forward; ratings are set each day from the forecast for that day."}]}]}}
